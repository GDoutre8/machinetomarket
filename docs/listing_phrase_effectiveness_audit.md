# Listing Phrase Effectiveness Audit

Research purpose: build the phrase intelligence layer for MTM Listing Copy Engine v3. This document classifies common equipment listing phrases by conversion value, proof requirements, overuse risk, and best use case.

This is not a blacklist. The goal is to decide when a phrase earns its place, what proof it needs, and how to translate it into buyer outcomes.

## Source Sampling

Public listing samples reviewed:

- MachineryTrader CTL/skid steer listings: examples included high-flow CTLs, "ready to work", "priced to sell fast", "job ready", low-hour units, and sparse spec-only dealer listings.
- MachineryTrader mini excavator listings: examples included hydraulic thumb, auxiliary hydraulics, track percentage, "work ready", "no leaks", bucket packages, and auction copy.
- MachineryTrader telehandler listings: examples included lift capacity, lift height, reach, forks, verified hours, and "job-ready" language.
- Equipment Trader listings: examples included financing disclosures, keyword-heavy SEO descriptions, private-property use claims, fresh service, and attachment packages.
- Boom & Bucket-style listings: examples included inspection summaries, "no active leaks", cold start/no smoke checks, oil analysis, digital glovebox, close-up photos, specs, and attachment inclusion notes.
- Ritchie List/dealer-style listings: examples included financing/freight/warranty availability, certified pre-owned claims, internal checklist language, and detailed excavator hydraulic/coupler descriptions.

## Methodology And Confidence

This audit weights sources by how directly they expose listing copy and proof structures.

| source_type | confidence | what_it_is_good_for | limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Full public listing pages | High | Exact phrase usage, field structure, proof language, attachment caveats | May be one seller/channel style |
| Search-indexed listing snippets | Medium | Pattern frequency, phrase repetition, channel conventions | Snippets are partial and can omit context |
| Inspection-report listings | High | Proof language, leak/code/function/oil-analysis wording | Not always sales-copy optimized |
| Dealer/OEM used inventory | High | Commercial support terms, warranty/protection fields, accessory tags | Often legal/offer-heavy |

| Facebook Marketplace proxy discussions | Medium/Low | Buyer anxieties, price/hour/photo/scam concerns, marketplace decision criteria | Not direct listing-copy corpus |

Working interpretation: phrase overuse is supported by repeated indexed listings and snippets; proof requirements are supported most strongly by inspection-report and dealer/OEM used-equipment sources.

## Part 2 - Phrase Effectiveness Audit

| phrase | classification | why_it_works_or_fails | evidence_needed | overuse_risk | best_use_case |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| low hours | B | Strong first-pass filter, especially when year/class benchmark supports it. Fails when used on machines that are only average for age. | `hours`, `year`, equipment-class threshold, optional verified meter reading | High | Lead or trust line for newer machines, premium dealer pages, marketplace hooks |
| one owner | B | Strong trust signal because it implies clearer history and less unknown use. Risky if undocumented. | `owner_count == 1` or `single_owner_verified == true` | Medium | Trust sentence after specs, local trade, dealer inventory |
| ready to work | C | Buyers understand it instantly, but it is saturated and often unsupported. | Minimum: known running condition. Strong: inspection passed, no active codes, no leaks, service current | Very high | Use only as closing support, not lead, unless inspection-backed |
| clean machine | D | Common but subjective; can mean cosmetics, cab, engine bay, or overall condition. | Photos, inspection notes, component-specific condition | Very high | Replace with specific proof: clean cab, dry engine bay, straight panels |
| contractor-owned | B | Can signal real-world use and practical maintenance, but also heavy use. Works only when source is known. | source/ownership note, maintenance history, application history | Medium | Trust/source line for honest working machines |
| high flow | A/B | High-converting for CTL/skid steer buyers because it unlocks attachment value. Safe if only stating confirmed feature; stronger with GPM. | `high_flow == yes`; best with `hi_flow_gpm` or `aux_flow_high_gpm` | Medium | CTL/skid steer lead, attachment package, forestry/mulching/planer/trencher fit |
| enclosed cab | A | Safe, concrete, visible, and buyer-relevant for comfort, weather, dust, and resale. | `cab_type == enclosed` or ROPS enclosed, plus heater/A/C fields if stated | Low | Feature block, marketplace scan line, premium comfort/value sentence |
| fresh service | B | Strong if specific; weak if generic. Buyers want date, hours, and work performed. | `service_date`, `service_hours`, `service_items`, invoice/record optional | High | Trust line for dealer-prepped units or recent maintenance |
| no leaks | B | Very strong remote-buyer trust signal, but high dispute risk. | `no_visible_leaks == true` from inspection or leak checklist | High | Inspection-backed trust line, Boom & Bucket-style proof block |
| no codes | B | High-value for emissions/electronics confidence, especially Tier 4 machines. | `no_active_fault_codes == true`, diagnostic scan date/tool | Medium | Trust line for inspected dealer units |
| turnkey | E/B | As generic copy it overpromises. Can be credible only when a full readiness package is proven. | inspection passed, no codes, no leaks, service current, included attachments confirmed | High | Avoid in generic engine; allow only for verified premium units |
| jobsite ready | C/B | Slightly better than "ready to work" because it implies practical deployment, but still needs proof. | Running condition; stronger with inspection/service/attachment inclusion | High | CTA or lead for inspected machines, telehandlers, CTLs, compact excavators |
| fleet maintained | B | Works when records exist; can backfire because rental/fleet use may imply harder duty cycles. | fleet source, service intervals, records attached, maintenance program | Medium | Dealer/fleet inventory with service documentation |
| financing available | A | Safe commercial support phrase if no approval or rate claim is implied. Reduces buyer friction. | dealer financing availability flag; terms required if rate/payment is stated | Medium | CTA/support line, dealer site, Equipment Trader, Ritchie-style listings |
| delivery available | A | Strong practical conversion aid for equipment buyers; low claim risk if service exists. | delivery/shipping availability flag; region/rate if specified | Medium | CTA/support line, remote buyer copy, dealer site |
| attachment package | A/B | High-value when it changes the deal economics and job fit. Risk is inclusion ambiguity. | `attachments_included` list and confirmation that pictured items are included | Medium | Lead/value block for mini excavators, CTLs, skid steers, telehandlers |

## Phrase Notes

- Highest-value phrases are concrete and mechanically meaningful: high flow, enclosed cab, hydraulic thumb, auxiliary hydraulics, lift capacity, lift height, dig depth, track/undercarriage percentage, included attachments.
- Highest-risk phrases are trust claims: no leaks, no codes, turnkey, fresh service, one owner, fleet maintained, like new, needs nothing.
- Weak phrases become useful when translated: "clean machine" becomes "clean cab, straight panels, dry engine bay" if inspection/photos support it.

## Deeper Corpus Findings

The second pass changed the emphasis more than the classifications. The same words appear everywhere; the differentiator is proof packaging.

| finding | observed_in | engine_takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| "Ready to work" is everywhere | MachineryTrader, Equipment Trader, AuctionTime | Treat as a low-rarity phrase. It should not carry the lead unless backed by inspection or service proof. |
| "Runs, drives, operates" is auction-native | AuctionTime, IronPlanet limited function checks | This is useful for Tier C/as-is copy because it is narrower than "ready to work." |
| "Sells as is" often coexists with positive running claims | AuctionTime | In auction mode, as-is disclosure should stay adjacent to running-state copy. |
| Inspection-led listings win credibility by naming components | IronPlanet, Boom & Bucket | Replace "clean machine" with component facts: dry engine bay, no dripping leaks, functional controls, track life %. |
| Boom & Bucket-style pages separate "what's great" and "what needs work" | Boom & Bucket | MTM should support positive and negative condition blocks instead of forcing one overall tone. |
| Attachment caveats are important | Boom & Bucket | Add a hard guardrail for pictured-but-unconfirmed attachments. |
| Cat/OEM dealer listings use commercial offer stacks | Cat Used | Financing/warranty/service offers should be gated by terms and rendered outside machine-condition copy. |
| Ritchie List telehandler copy is capacity-first | Ritchie List | Telehandler leads should prioritize capacity/height/reach before comfort or generic condition. |
| High-flow CTL/skid copy is strongest when paired with GPM | Ritchie List, MachineryTrader, Cat Used | Render "high flow" safely when confirmed; render attachment outcomes only with GPM or known attachment fit. |
| "Low hours" appears even on moderate-hour units | MachineryTrader, Equipment Trader, AuctionTime | Require class/year benchmark to avoid credibility loss. |

## Facebook Marketplace Proxy Findings

Direct Facebook Marketplace listing research remains limited because search and listing access are heavily session, location, and login gated. The useful public evidence came from buyer discussions referencing Facebook Marketplace equipment searches and risks. Treat this as proxy research, not a scraped FB corpus.

| proxy_signal | observed_pattern | MTM implication |
|---|---|---|
| Scam concern is unusually high | Buyers warn against equipment listings where seller cannot show machine in person or proof is thin | Marketplace copy should emphasize verifiable identity, local inspection, walkaround video, serial/stock, and dealer contact consistency |
| Price/hours dominate first-pass judgment | Buyer discussions compare mini excavators by brand, model, asking price, and hours within a local radius | FB-style copy should put price, year/make/model, hours, and location high in the listing |
| Cheap imported mini excavators create trust anxiety | Buyer threads focus on parts support, hydraulic weakness, resale value, and reliability | If selling off-brand/import units, copy should avoid big claims and include parts/service support facts if available |
| High flow is buyer-understood but attachment-specific | Buyer advice frames high flow as necessary only for certain tools like brush cutters, hammers, and mulchers | Do not oversell high flow; translate only to compatible attachment categories |
| Track/tire repair cost is top-of-mind | Buyer discussions call out tracks/tires as expensive ownership items | Track/tire percent and photos should be treated as high-value marketplace proof |
| Photos and inspection access matter more than polished prose | Marketplace buyers are wary of remote equipment scams | Marketplace CTA should invite a walkaround, inspection, or video call rather than rely on hype |

Marketplace-specific high-confidence rule: FB-style copy should be shorter, more concrete, and more proof-forward than dealer-site copy. It should not read like a brochure.

## Part 4 - Engine Guardrails

Deterministic rules for MTM Listing Copy Engine v3:

1. Never render a trust-sensitive phrase unless all required claims pass.
2. If a required evidence field is missing, omit the phrase; do not downgrade it into vague positivity.
3. Treat dealer-entered condition notes as disclosure input, not proof of positive mechanical claims.
4. "Low hours" requires hours plus an equipment-type threshold or benchmark. Otherwise render factual hours only.
5. "One owner" requires ownership count or explicit verified single-owner flag.
6. "Fresh service" requires service date or service hours plus items performed.
7. "No leaks" requires inspection-backed leak status.
8. "No codes" requires diagnostic scan or telematics-backed code status.
9. "Turnkey" requires inspection passed, no active codes, no visible leaks, service current, and included work attachment confirmed.
10. "Ready to work" and "jobsite ready" may not appear in the opening lead unless inspection or readiness proof exists.
11. If an attachment appears in photos but is not confirmed included, copy must not imply inclusion.
12. Attachment lists must use "included" only when `attachments_included` confirms the item.
13. Feature fields and attachment fields must remain separate: high flow is a machine capability; mulcher is an attachment.
14. High-flow buyer-value translations require high-flow confirmation; GPM-specific translation requires GPM.
15. If high flow is "optional" or unknown, do not render "high flow equipped."
16. Financing language may say available only when the dealer supports it; rates, payments, or approval language require terms.
17. Delivery/shipping language may say available only when the dealer supports it; geographic promises require region data.
18. Auction copy must disclose known limitations early, including non-runner, AC not working, codes, leaks, worn undercarriage, or missing title/serial issues.
19. Tier C machines should use honest issue-forward copy and avoid readiness claims.
20. Tier B machines can use factual work-fit language but not premium trust claims without proof.
21. Tier A should be reserved for inspection-backed machines with service and condition proof.
22. Platform compression must not remove known issues, missing attachment caveats, or important trust qualifiers.
23. CTA should match missing proof: if attachments are unclear, CTA asks to confirm attachments; if remote buyer, CTA offers walkaround/shipping.
24. Avoid repeated lead templates within the same inventory batch by deterministic rotation.
25. Prefer one strong proof-backed claim over three generic claims.
